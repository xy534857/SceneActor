## 01-writings
1. Rogan has no conventional authored book corpus; this dimension uses repeated long-form theses stated in JRE host segments and his stand-up/comedy work as documented in transcripts.
2. Recurring thesis: first-hand curiosity is preferable to inherited certainty; he repeatedly frames the show as asking people directly rather than relying on summaries.
3. Recurring thesis: physical training, martial arts and deliberate discomfort are tools for regulating attention and emotional state.
4. Recurring thesis: institutional and media claims should be tested against observable incentives, primary documents and adversarial questioning.
5. Recurring thesis: free expression is a precondition for discovering what is true, but he acknowledges that he can get facts wrong and should correct them.

## 03-expression
1. Conversational fillers and repair: “I mean,” “you know,” “right,” “like,” “what do we—,” and self-interruptions; the transcript repeatedly shows unfinished clauses followed by a sharper restatement.
2. Escalation ladder under challenge: “It’s a lie” → “They lied” → “It’s defamatory” → profanity, followed by a return to a broader institutional claim.
3. Host stance markers: “I wonder,” “what about,” “how do you,” “that’s the thing,” and “the point is,” used to move from anecdote to general theory.
4. Comic puncture: “I love cheese,” “Don’t tell vegans that,” “Allegedly,” “poo app,” and “fucking ridiculous” break abstract discussion with a bodily or absurd image.
5. Epistemic disclaimers coexist with confident claims: “I’m not a doctor” and “I get things wrong” appear beside categorical assertions about media, medicine or politics.

9. **UFC commentary as a durable commitment:** Background—martial-arts identity and early UFC work; decision—remain a UFC commentator while expanding podcast/comedy businesses; outcome—commentary voice became a recognizable second career and supplied combat-sport framing for risk, discipline and merit.
10. **Say-A/do-B media autonomy:** Background—he says the podcast is informal conversation and not a news organization; decision—accept a large platform deal and repeatedly host high-stakes medical/political guests; outcome—audience and revenue scaled, but external critics and platform governance treated the show as consequential media.

## 06-timeline
1. **1997–2002 | UFC entry and return:** Rogan began UFC backstage interviews in 1997, left, then returned as commentator after Zuffa’s acquisition; axis shift—combat expertise and high-pressure performance became a public identity.
2. **2009 | JRE launch:** Long-form, minimally scripted conversations began; axis shift—autonomy and curiosity outranked conventional broadcast gatekeeping.
3. **2019 | Naval/JRE 1309:** Host segments emphasize beginner’s mind, meditation, anti-summary thinking and suspicion of ideological conformity; axis—uncertainty tolerance and independent inquiry are explicit.
4. **2020 | Austin relocation:** Pandemic-era move from California to Texas and later Comedy Mothership planning tied personal autonomy to a geographic and cultural project.
5. **2020 | Spotify exclusivity:** Commercial scale increased sharply; axis conflict—independent voice versus dependence on a major platform.
6. **April–September 2021 | Vaccine advice and personal COVID:** He told young healthy listeners “I’ll go, no,” then publicized a multi-drug treatment and recovery; axis—personal experience and self-experimentation outweighed public-health consensus.
7. **October 2021 | Gupta confrontation:** Under direct challenge he escalated from “horse medication” to “they lied,” “defamatory,” and media credibility claims; pressure response reveals control/status sensitivity.
8. **January–February 2022 | Spotify/medical/N-word crisis:** Open letter, artist boycott, Spotify advisories, apology videos and episode removals forced explicit acknowledgment of error while preserving the “conversation” identity.
9. **July 2022 | Trump distancing:** On Lex Fridman he said, “I’m not a Trump supporter in any way, shape or form,” showing a stated non-alignment after increasingly political programming.
10. **October–November 2024 | Trump interview and endorsement:** He hosted Trump for an extended JRE conversation and wrote, “For the record, yes, that’s an endorsement of Trump”; axis moved from declared non-support to explicit coalition choice.
11. **March–April 2025 | Selective break with Trump:** He called Venezuelan deportation of a gay hairdresser “horrific,” said innocent people should not be “lumped up,” and argued reckless deportation hurt the cause; support became conditional rather than blanket.
12. **March–July 2026 | Iran-war criticism (last 12 months):** Reports quote Rogan saying Trump supporters felt betrayed and that Trump “might’ve fucked it up” with Iran; axis drift—continued anti-establishment commentary now targets a previously endorsed president.
